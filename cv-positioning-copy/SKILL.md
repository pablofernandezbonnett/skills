# CV Positioning Copy

## Overview

Use this skill when working on a CV, LinkedIn profile, cover letter, or candidate
summary that must stay aligned with verified career evidence while making the
candidate's strengths easy to understand.

Default goal:
- preserve credibility
- improve clarity and punch
- avoid buzzword-heavy or AI-optimized wording
- keep all outputs aligned with verified candidate evidence

## Workflow

1. Start from the source of truth.
   Find the candidate's canonical profile or fact base in the current project.
   If none exists, use only facts supported by the material the user provided
   and flag material gaps before making claims that depend on them.

2. Confirm what kind of artifact you are editing.
   Common cases:
   - `master CV`
   - offer-specific CV
   - LinkedIn profile
   - cover letter

3. Keep the same truth, change only the framing.
   Do not invent a new narrative per document. Reweight emphasis based on the artifact and role.

4. Prefer proof over wording.
   Replace abstract claims with concrete systems, flows, stakeholders, integrations, or responsibilities.

5. Keep technology, project, and exposure levels precise.
   Distinguish a technology used from a product delivered, and participation
   from ownership.

6. Keep situational seniority visible when it is real.
   If the candidate was trusted to mentor, coordinate day-to-day work, unblock others, or act as the practical bridge with stakeholders, preserve that signal without upgrading it into a formal leadership title.

7. Preserve the candidate's actual professional focus without hiding relevant
   adjacent work. Let the evidence establish their primary area and the breadth
   that supports it.

8. Do not infer customer market, working language, or product exposure from the
   candidate's physical location alone.

9. If the wording starts to sound generic, loaded, or too optimized, simplify it.
   The target tone is direct, human, and interview-defendable.

## Document Rules

### Master CV

- Optimize for fast recruiter understanding.
- Keep the identity stable.
- Preserve the strongest evidence near the top.
- Compress older roles before weakening strong recent roles.
- If space is tight, remove redundancy before removing signals.
- Keep informal leadership signals when they were real and repeated, but phrase them as situational trust or senior support rather than formal management.
- Keep the candidate's evidenced professional focus visible alongside relevant
  adjacent work.

### LinkedIn

- Keep it broader than the CV, but not less truthful.
- Let the `About` read naturally.
- Keep the candidate's evidenced professional focus central while allowing
  broader context than the CV.

### Cover Letters

- Start from role fit, not autobiography.
- Use only experience that clearly maps to the company or role pressure.
- Do not claim missing depth just because the job description asks for it.

## Source-of-Truth Conflicts

Treat the candidate profile and verified work history as evidence to reconcile,
not a ranking where one silently overrides the other. If they conflict, flag
the discrepancy and avoid the disputed claim until it is resolved. For
framing choices that do not change the facts, prefer market positioning over
job-description keyword coverage.
